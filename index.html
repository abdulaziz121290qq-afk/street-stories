Why street food?

Food on the street is never just food

Street stalls sit where language, money, memory and politics meet. Every name shouted from a cart carries a story about who belongs and who is changing.

01
🗣️

Language

Dish names, vendor calls and bilingual menus show how communities label themselves for locals and tourists.

02
🏮

Tradition

Recipes passed between generations turn the sidewalk into a living archive of cultural heritage.

03
🌏

Globalization

Fast-food chains, social media and tourism reshape what is cooked, how it is sold and who it is for.

04
⚖️

Power

City rules, class and gender decide who may cook in public space and whose food is called “authentic”.

The big question

Can a dish stay local in a global world?

When pad thai is served in New York and bánh mì enters the Oxford English Dictionary, who still owns the recipe? When a Tashkent somsa stand sits next to a burger chain, what changes in how young people eat — and speak about food?

Our project follows these questions through history, sociology and language, using real cases from Uzbekistan, Thailand, Vietnam and China.
