Reflection 5 of 13

Kamola

Research · Part A

Wrote Part A: the project introduction, the About page and the team introductions.

Key learnings

Writing the introduction required me to explain why food deserves academic study at all. Mintz and Du Bois (2002) gave me the answer: food connects to almost every question about society, from class to memory to nation. Douglas (1972) showed that a meal has a structure like a sentence, with rules about what comes first and what belongs together.

I applied this to an Uzbek table. Bread is broken first, tea is poured by the host and palov comes at the end. Nobody explains these rules, yet everyone follows them. That is what a food code is.

Personal motto

My guiding phraseA meal is a sentence that everyone at the table can read.

It helps me notice the unspoken rules in ordinary situations, and to be patient when I am the guest who does not know them yet.

Applying course themes

I will pay more attention to table customs when I meet people from other cultures, and ask about them instead of assuming. Introducing our team taught me the same thing: every member brought different food experiences, and the project became richer because of it.
