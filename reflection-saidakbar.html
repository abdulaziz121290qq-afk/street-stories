Research · Part D

Wrote Part D with Behroz and Yakub: the Project Conclusion, Key Insights and the Discussion blog.

Key learnings

The concept I found most convincing is banal nationalism (Billig, 1995): the nation is kept alive through small daily reminders that we hardly notice. Ichijo and Ranta (2016) apply this to food. A national dish on every street does more for identity than a speech.

I thought about how often I see a qozon of palov in Tashkent, on the street, at weddings and on television. UNESCO’s recognition (UNESCO, 2016) made this official, but the everyday repetition is what makes it feel natural.

Personal motto

My guiding phraseIdentity is what we repeat every day.

Big symbols matter less than habits. What we eat on an ordinary Thursday says more about us than what we serve on a holiday.

Applying course themes

I now notice everyday symbols of identity around me, in food and beyond it. In my blog post I applied this to language, asking why new food places choose English names and what that repetition might do over time.

Sources on this page

  • Ichijo, A., & Ranta, R. (2016). Food, national identity and nationalism: From everyday to global politics. Palgrave Macmillan.
