Research · Part C (3–4)

Researched and wrote Part C, chapters 3–4: Sociocultural & Sociopolitical Context and Global Reception.

Key learnings

My chapters were about power. Reading about vendor evictions in Bangkok (Boonjubun, 2017), bans in Hanoi (Turner & Schoenberger, 2012) and street conflicts in China (Hanser, 2016), I realised that the right to sell food in public is political. The same governments that advertise street food to tourists also remove the people who make it.

Heldke (2003) then turned the question on me. As a tourist I also want cheap, “authentic” food, and I rarely think about the conditions of the person cooking it.

Personal motto

My guiding phraseThere is no street food without the street.

It reminds me that culture needs physical space and legal space. If vendors lose their place, the food becomes a memory or a product.

Applying course themes

I will pay attention to how my own city treats small sellers when markets are rebuilt or streets are “improved”. As a customer and a traveller, I want to choose vendors over copies of them, and to treat them as skilled workers.
