Key concepts

The words behind the food

Twenty concepts from the course and from our research, explained briefly. Type in the box to filter.

✎Prepared by Research team

Authenticity

The belief that a food is “real” or true to its origin. Scholars treat it as a judgement made by people, not a fixed property of a dish (Johnston & Baumann, 2010).

Banal nationalism

Everyday, unnoticed reminders of the nation, such as a national dish sold on every corner (Billig, 1995).

Chengguan (城管)

Urban management officers in Chinese cities whose duties include policing street vendors (Hanser, 2016).

Code-switching

Moving between two or more languages in one conversation, common when ordering food in multilingual cities such as Tashkent.

Culinary nationalism

Using food to define and promote a nation, at home and abroad (Ichijo & Ranta, 2016).

Distinction

Bourdieu’s (1984) idea that taste in food and culture marks and reproduces social class.

Đổi Mới

Vietnam’s economic reforms from 1986, which allowed private trade and a boom in street vending.

Food adventurer

Heldke’s (2003) term for eaters who collect “exotic” cuisines as experiences, sometimes without respect for the cooks.

Foodscape

All the places and ways in which people get and eat food in a city (Yasmeen, 2006).

Foodways

The whole pattern of how a group produces, prepares, shares and thinks about food.

Gastrodiplomacy

Government use of national cuisine to improve a country’s image abroad (Rockower, 2012).

Glocalization

The local adaptation of global products and ideas, producing mixture instead of sameness (Robertson, 1995).

Incorporation

Fischler’s (1988) principle that people feel they become what they eat, which links food to identity.

Informal economy

Work that is not fully registered or protected by the state. Most street vending belongs here (Tinker, 1997).

Intangible cultural heritage

Living traditions recognised by UNESCO, such as Uzbekistan’s palov culture (UNESCO, 2016).

Linguistic landscape

The visible written language in public space: signs, menus and notices (Landry & Bourhis, 1997).

Managed informality

Unofficial but organised toleration of street vendors by local officials (Batréau & Bonnet, 2016).

McDonaldization

Ritzer’s (1993) term for the spread of fast-food principles: efficiency, calculability, predictability and control.

Tandir

A clay oven used across Central Asia. Bread and somsa are baked on its inner wall.

Xiaochi (小吃)

“Small eats”: the Chinese term for snacks and street foods, often tied to a region (Swislocki, 2009).
